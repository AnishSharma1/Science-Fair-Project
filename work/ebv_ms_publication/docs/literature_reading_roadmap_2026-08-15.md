# EBV–MS manuscript literature reading roadmap

**Search date:** 2026-08-15  
**Purpose:** learn how strong papers in this field build an argument while assembling the evidence base for the manuscript.  
**Corpus:** 51 papers, divided into close-reading exemplars, scientific background, mechanism, structural methods, and claim-boundary papers.

## How to use this list

Do not read all 51 papers linearly. Deep-read papers 1–12 and use papers 13–51 as section-specific references.

For every close read, record:

1. The paper's one-sentence research question.
2. The job performed by each Introduction paragraph.
3. The order of the Results subsections and why that order works.
4. The exact evidence supporting each major claim.
5. Where the authors shift from result to interpretation.
6. The verbs used to limit causality: *associated*, *supported*, *consistent with*, *suggested*, or *demonstrated*.
7. One sentence pattern or transition worth imitating structurally—never copying verbatim.

## Recommended reading order

### Pass 1 — Learn the field's narrative

Read 1, 13, 3, and 28. You should then be able to explain why EBV and HLA-DR15 are convergent risk factors and why DRB1*15:01 and DRB5*01:01 cannot be treated as interchangeable.

### Pass 2 — Learn how a molecular-mimicry claim is earned

Read 2, 10, 11, 5, and 21. Track which papers provide sequence similarity, structural similarity, receptor cross-recognition, functional activation, patient evidence, or animal evidence. Do not collapse these layers.

### Pass 3 — Learn computational Methods and limitations writing

Read 12, 34, 36, 38, and 40. Compare their benchmark sets, unit of analysis, negative controls, confidence language, and failure reporting with this project's frozen-control audit.

### Pass 4 — Model the manuscript's tone

Re-read 6, 7, and 18. Study how the authors report useful but incomplete evidence and how their Discussion separates findings, interpretation, limitations, and next experiments.

## Search and selection notes

- PubMed was searched across EBV–MS epidemiology, EBV molecular mimicry, HLA-DR15 antigen presentation, class-II structural cross-reactivity, and AlphaFold/TCR-pMHC benchmarking.
- Results were deduplicated by PubMed identity and screened for relevance to the current manuscript rather than citation count alone.
- Records dated after 2026-08-15 were excluded even if already indexed.
- Reviews are included for orientation and writing style, but primary studies should support factual manuscript claims.
- Computational or animal studies are labeled by purpose and should not be cited as direct evidence of human TCR cross-reactivity or MS mechanism.
